# Fire industry ops — v0.1

How to put fire out, what a crew will actually accept, and what we get wrong if we invent the fire part. Airframe, BOM, and sim live elsewhere.

## The foothold

v0.1 is not a wildfire. Prove firefighting on a prescribed pile or a leftover hotspot next to a Type 4/5 crew, under existing air ops.

Success is three things a burn boss already understands:

1. Find the heat.
2. Put water or Class A on it.
3. Hand them a DDM pin they can punch into Avenza or a Garmin they already carry.

Miss the heat, or give them a pin they do not trust, and we do not have a v0.1. We sit next to the crew. We do not replace them.

## Why a prescribed pile / leftover hotspot, not a wildfire

A wildfire is the wrong first customer.

- Incident command will not put an unknown bird into a going fire next to helicopters and air attack.
- Crown fire, deep flame, and heavy smoke are outside what a small UAS can legally or usefully treat.
- Water volume that fits Part 107 wet (about 1.5–2 gal) cannot knock down a head. It can treat isolated leftover heat.

A prescribed pile or leftover hotspot is the job a backpack pump already does: wet mop-up on a known, small, low-intensity target, with a burn boss on the radio and a Type 4/5 crew standing there. That is a foothold. A demo over a roaring pile, or a claim we will "put the pile out," is a toy.

Mop-up is water plus a hand tool. The bird puts water on the heat so the crew does not have to walk into it. The crew still stirs and checks. We do not replace the stir.

## Who we sit next to

- **Burn boss / IC** owns yes/no. One human on our radio, talking to them, in their language (heat, pin, gallons, not "autonomy").
- **Type 4/5 crew** is already on the unit with bladder bags, hand tools, and maybe an engine. We look like a flying backpack pump, not a new ICS box.
- **Existing air ops** own the sky. Helicopters, helitack, and any TFR come first. We stay out of their way, daylight, VLOS, and we do not fly in rotor wash or the smoke column.

Federal and most state fire will not let a no-name or non-NDAA bird over a pile next to their air ops. US / NDAA-path is an ops requirement, not a branding preference.

## What a burn boss will say yes to

They will say yes to leftover heat / mop-up assist on a prescribed pile if all of this is true:

- Water or Class A (0.1–0.3% wet foam). Not retardant. Retardant is logistics and a regulatory headache we do not need for v0.1.
- One human on the radio.
- A pin they already trust (DDM they can drop into Avenza or a Garmin). Before/after thermal they can see.
- The drop lands on the heat, as a coarse stream/slug, not a farm mist.
- The bird is US / NDAA-path, Part 107, and looks like a tool, not a demo.

They will not say yes to "we'll put out the pile," a swarm story, a DJI over their air ops, or a bird that needs a new app to read the pin.

## Why a bought DJI is a dead end

Cost pushed a stock Agras T50. Fire ops vetoes it.

Federal and most state fire will not put DJI over a pile next to their air ops. NDAA / Blue UAS / data security is a procurement gate, not a nice-to-have. San Bernardino's FireSparrow is sold as NDAA-compliant for a reason. A cheap T50 that a burn boss cannot accept is not a foothold.

Build US, or a path that can get there. Do not invent the fire part to justify the airframe: same water or Class A, same one pass on leftover heat, same one human on the radio.

## What we would get wrong if we invented the fire part

**Treating volume like the product.** Helibuckets drop hundreds of gallons and lay line. We have about 2 gal under Part 107 wet. That is bladder-bag work (a 5-gal backpack pump is the tool they already trust), done from the air, at cadence. One 2-gal dump on a roaring pile edge is a splash. Isolated leftover heat, hit accurately, is real work. UMD fire-protection work put out a ~0.6 MW target with 8.5 kg (~2.2 gal) when the water arrived as a slug, not a mist.

**Farm spray.** Stock ag swaths (4–11 m at 3 m) and 100–300 µm droplets evaporate in the plume. Coarse stream/slug, sheets that break into ~1–6 mm drops. Hansen's critical rate is about 0.013 kg/m²·s with no external flux — 2 gal on 1–2 m² of isolated heat clears that if it actually lands. Radiant feedback from a live pile eats a single dump.

**A fast airplane pass.** 2 gal at 16 m/s for 1–2 s is a ~30 m streak at coverage level ~0.3, below grass. That is a toy. The signed dump is a tilt-crawl crossing, 3–4 m/s, 1 s stream, ~4 m streak, centroid in the middle third of the patch. An airplane pass stays off.

**Hover-until-empty over the core.** The bird cannot sit feet-on-pin (about 0.6 m of lean). Do not hover in an 80°C plume. Upwind crossing, airframe offset, dump on the way through.

**Timing the door instead of the ground.** A 2 m lead at 8 m AGL throws the slug past the pin. Prove-it is **8 m over the crown (not the dirt), 6 m lead, no-wind.** Sky-eye aims predicted ground impact with wind. Step to 5 m over-crown only after the first hits land; lead there is still ~5–6 m.

**Calling "somewhere in the 4 m streak" a hit.** A graze on the wrong side of the log is a miss. The thermal centroid has to sit in the middle third (within about 1 m of dump center).

**Inventing chemistry, ICS, or a wildfire.** No custom retardant, no new command box, no "we fight wildfire in v0.1." Water or Class A, one radio, one pin, leftover heat next to a crew that is already there.

## Signed prove-it (ops)

| Item | Lock |
| --- | --- |
| Target | Prescribed pile leftover hotspot / mop-up only |
| Crew | Type 4/5 + burn boss, existing air ops |
| Agent | Water or Class A 0.1–0.3%, stream not spray |
| Volume | 1.5–2.0 gal (about 8 L) per sortie |
| Geometry | Tilt-crawl 3–4 m/s, 1 s dump, ~4 m streak |
| Height | 8 m over crown prove-it; 5 m over crown after first hits |
| Lead | 6 m at 8 m (no-wind); ~5–6 m at 5 m |
| Aim | Centroid in middle third of the patch |
| Cue | Sky-eye predicted ground impact + wind; DDM pin they already trust |
| Radio | One human to the burn boss |
| Dead | Wildfire, DJI, airplane pass, farm mist, hover-until-empty, feet-on-pin, 2 m lead |

If we cannot show steam, a thermal drop, and a pin that stays cold long enough for the crew to walk over and stir, we did not prove the system.
